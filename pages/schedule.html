Interest is charged on what is still owed, for the actual number of days between instalments — so a longer gap costs more, and the first instalment costs most of all. Dates falling on a weekend are marked; the schedule keeps the calendar date, because that is what the contract says, and the borrower pays the next working day. Saving stores the contract, not the rows — the figures are worked out again each time it is opened, so a saved schedule can never disagree with its own arithmetic.
